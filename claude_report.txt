# Executive Reputation Diagnostic

**Subject:** Divyank "Div" Turakhia, Founder, Ai.tech (Dubai)
**Profile assessed:** linkedin.com/in/divyankturakhia
**Date:** 15 September 2026
**Claims tested:** 16 | **Verified:** 6 | **Partially verified:** 6 | **Unverified:** 2 | **Contradicted:** 2

**Headline:** The record behind this profile is unusually strong. The profile itself is not. Two numbers on it are contradicted by primary sources, three of its most repeated superlatives trace back to a single 2016 press release, and the profile contradicts itself in four places. None of this is fatal. All of it is findable by a DIFC diligence analyst in an afternoon.

---

## How claims were graded

Sources are tiered before they are counted, because two sources that copied each other are one source.

| Tier | What counts | Examples used here |
|---|---|---|
| **P — Primary** | Regulatory filing, registry record, government page, acquirer's own disclosure | Endurance International SEC Form 10-Q and 8-K, ICANN records, UAE Ministry of Economy & Tourism |
| **S — Secondary** | Named reporting with editorial standards, not a press-release rewrite | Forbes India, TechCrunch, CNBC Make It, Domain Name Wire, Business Standard |
| **T — Tertiary** | Wikipedia, wikis, aggregators, subject's own sites, speaker bios, LinkedIn | Wikipedia, ICANNWiki, Crunchbase, directi.com, radix.website, Milken speaker bio |

**Verified** requires two sources that do not share an origin, at least one of them P or S.
**Partially verified** means the substance holds but a detail is wrong, or corroboration exists only at T.
**Unverified** means one source, or only self-originating sources.
**Contradicted** means a P or S source says otherwise.

## The claim the system refused to pass through

**Refused: "Directi ... culminating in a $160M exit in 2014."**

This one matters because it would have survived almost any verification method. It has more than two sources. It has sources in different countries. It appears in Forbes-adjacent trade press, on Wikipedia, on Directi's own LinkedIn company page and on the family's own websites. A dual-source check clears it instantly.

It is still wrong. The buyer filed the number with the SEC and the number is $109.8M. Every one of those independent-looking sources is downstream of the same 2016 Inc42 and YourStory write-ups, which took it from the same place.

The rule this produced: **for any transaction involving a listed company, the counterparty's filing outranks the entire rest of the internet, including the subject's own website.** Source count is not evidence. Source independence is, and independence has to be traced, not assumed.

**Also held back for publication:** the 2023 Media.net SSP re-acquisition, and the "net worth over $1B at 33" line. Neither would be published under any circumstances until documented.

## The three biggest gaps in how he currently shows up

**1. The verifiable record ends in 2018. The current venture has almost none.**
Everything independently documented about him (the Media.net sale, the CEO handover, the Forbes and Hurun rankings, the CNBC profile) sits between 2014 and 2018. Ai.tech, founded January 2022 and now his primary vehicle, has essentially one independent press moment: the September 2025 Hurun unicorn list, which is itself company-supplied. He has 6,190 followers. For a man Hurun places among the hundred richest Indians, that is not a small presence, it is an absence. The public story reads in past tense, and it is the wrong tense for someone raising, hiring and partnering in AI right now.

**2. The load-bearing superlatives are circular, and the circle closes on one press release.**
"Top five ad tech company by market cap." "Second-largest contextual advertising business by revenue." "Third-largest ad-tech deal." All three trace to Media.net's own 2016 and 2018 communications. They have since been laundered through Wikipedia and a UAE government page, which makes them look independently sourced. They are not. Any counterparty who traces one traces all three, and the collapse takes the credible parts of the paragraph down with it. The fix is not to drop them. It is to attribute them and lead with the figures that survive scrutiny: $232M revenue in 2015, 800 to 1,300 employees, $900M consideration, all of which hold.

**3. The profile contradicts itself, and contradicts his own companies' sites, in ways that read as carelessness at best.**
Skenzo is 2005–2008 in the About and 2005–2018 in Experience. Media.net is 2010–2016 in the About and 2010–2018 in Experience. Net worth crosses $1B at 33 on LinkedIn and at 34 on directi.com. He is listed as "Founder" of Directi and of Radix while the body text of the same profile says co-founded, and while Radix's own site credits his brother as the one who started it. Forbes India has always ranked the brothers as one entry. In DIFC, where LPs and regulators run exactly this check, a unilateral "Founder" tag on a company his brother chairs is the highest-cost, lowest-benefit line on the page.
